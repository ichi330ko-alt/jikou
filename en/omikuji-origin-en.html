The Story of Tsuki Omikuji

Tsuki Omikuji began with a single poem about movement through time: appearing, growing, connecting, losing, seeking, realizing, and finally entrusting what cannot be held.

It is not a traditional shrine omikuji. It is an original oracle created within JIKŌ in Japan.

The Mother Poem

One poem, eighteen scenes

生まれ 育ち 姿をあらわし ひらき 結ばれ 満ち 実り 保たれ 巡り また求め 問い 迷い 阻まれ 失い 枯れ 力及ばず やがて気づき 祈る

English rendering

We are born, we grow, we come into view, we open, we connect, we become full, we bear fruit, we preserve, we circulate. Then we seek again, we question, we wander, we are obstructed, we lose, we wither, and reach what our own strength cannot change. In time, we realize— and we pray.

The English is intentionally a rendering rather than a word-for-word replacement. The Japanese poem remains the source text.

The Structure

18 scenes × 14 responses = 252 oracles

The eighteen lines of the mother poem became eighteen scenes—states in which a person may find themselves. Each scene is then met by one of fourteen possible responses.

18 Scenes

Emergence, growth, revelation, opening, connection, fullness, fruition, preservation, circulation, seeking, inquiry, uncertainty, blockage, loss, withering, powerlessness, realization, and prayer.

14 Responses

Begin, move forward, continue, wait, protect, step back, release, choose, restore order, change, connect, receive, seek, and reflect.

252 Combinations

A scene describes where you are. A response suggests how you might meet that place. Together they form one oracle.

How to Read It

Not a command. A point of reflection.

Tsuki Omikuji is not intended to decide your future or replace your judgment. Before drawing, hold one matter quietly in mind. The oracle gives you a scene and a response through which to look at it.

Some messages encourage movement. Others suggest waiting, protecting, releasing, receiving, or simply seeing more clearly. A difficult scene is not a “bad fortune,” and an abundant scene is not a guarantee. The value is in the relationship between the present state and the response.

Why 月御籤?

The moon changes without ceasing to be the moon

The name 月御籤 — Tsuki Omikuji literally joins the moon (月) with omikuji (御籤). The moon was chosen because its visible form changes continually while its existence remains.

JIKŌ looks at human experience in a similar way: not as a fixed label, but as movement through changing phases. The oracle is therefore offered as a message for the phase you are standing in now.
